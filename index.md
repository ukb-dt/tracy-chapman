### 1. The Blueprint: Tracy Chapman - "Fast Car"
This is the progression that launched a thousand ships. It is deceptively simple but harmonically ambiguous, which is why it feels like it's constantly moving but never arriving.
* **The Chords (Relative to Capo 2):** `Cmaj7 – G – Em – D`
* **The Theory:** `IV – I – vi – V`
* **The Vibe:** It starts on the **IV chord** (Subdominant), which creates immediate tension—like a car already in motion. It resolves to the **I** (Home), drops to the minor **vi** (Sadness), and ends on the **V** (Tension), forcing you to loop back to the start. It is a perfect musical circle of "going nowhere."

### 2. The Direct Descendant: Babyface - "When Can I See You"
Babyface takes the "acoustic loop" concept but straightens it out into a classic pop ballad structure.
* **The Chords:** `G – Am7 – C – D`
* **The Theory:** `I – ii – IV – V`
* **The Comparison:** Notice the shared DNA? He uses the same **G, C, and D** palette as Chapman. But instead of Chapman’s circular, unresolved `IV-I` start, Babyface gives you a linear `I-ii-IV` climb. It’s less "desperate escape" and more "gentle longing." He simplified Chapman's folk ambiguity into R&B certainty.

### 3. The Genetic Splice: Toni Braxton - "Let It Flow"
Here is where Babyface gets clever. He wrote this for the *Waiting to Exhale* soundtrack, and the progression literally "exhales" downwards.
* **The Chords:** `Cmaj7 – Am7 – Em7 – Dm7`
* **The Theory:** `I – vi – iii – ii`
* **The Genealogy:**
    * **The Texture:** It uses the exact same **Maj7** and **min7** warmth as "Fast Car."
    * **The Motion:** While "Fast Car" loops, "Let It Flow" **descends**. It tumbles down the scale from the 1, to the 6, to the 3, to the 2.
    * **The Inference:** If "Fast Car" is about driving in circles, "Let It Flow" is about gravity—letting the weight fall off your shoulders.

### The Verdict: The "Acoustic Soul" Lineage
You can infer that Babyface heard "Fast Car" and realized that **emotion doesn't need complex jazz chords.** He took Chapman's "folk logic"—using open, ringing acoustic chords (C, G, D, Em)—and applied it to R&B.
